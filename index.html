      About the data and method

      Scores for the 44 conference games played through Oct. 31 come from published game reports. One widely used table listed LSU–Ole Miss as 34–26; the correct final, 32–24, is used here.

      Rules follow the SEC tiebreaker document (Steps A–F). Before relying on an edge case, confirm the current version on secsports.com.

      Standings rank by conference winning percentage using decided games only. Unpicked games are ignored, so a partial projection can shift as you keep picking.

      The document governs championship-game spots only. Below second place, tied teams are ordered by head-to-head when it separates them, the same way Step C orders opponents; otherwise they share a rank.

      Step E is an approximation. SportSource Analytics computes it from full-season scoring averages. This tool uses season-to-date averages from completed non-conference games plus every conference game with a score, applies the 200% offensive cap, and rounds each component to 0.1 as in the Appendix A example. Picked games without a score are left out and flagged. Add a projected score to any game to include it.

      Step F is simulated with a repeatable random draw; use Redraw to try another. The team that wins a tiebreaker for first takes the No. 1 seed and is the home team.

      Projections: each remaining game shows ESPN’s FPI win probability as published by the outlet and date listed below. ESPN updates FPI daily, so figures from different dates are mixed; seven predate the Oct. 31 games and are marked in orange. For four games no published figure turned up, so the probability is estimated from ESPN’s post-Week 9 FPI team ratings with P(home) = Φ((ratinghome − ratingaway + 2.2) / 14). That formula reproduces the 13 published post-Week 9 figures within 0.9 points on average (largest miss 1.9). Florida’s rating is bounded between Missouri’s and Texas A&M’s from its rank.

      Race to Atlanta: odds come from 10,000 simulated finishes in which each unpicked game is decided by its FPI probability, then run through the full tiebreaker procedure. With 10,000 runs, a 50% figure carries about ±1 point of sampling error. Your picks and the finals are treated as settled.

      Clinched and eliminated are exact, not estimated: a team is eliminated only if no combination of unpicked results sends it to Atlanta, and clinched only if every combination does. Because Step E needs scores from games not yet played and Step F is a draw, any tie that reaches those steps counts as able to go either way. “Controls own destiny” means winning all its unpicked games guarantees a spot. If a case can’t be settled in a few seconds it is labeled pending rather than guessed.
