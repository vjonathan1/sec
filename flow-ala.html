    How this was calculated

    Each outcome was run through the full SEC tiebreaker procedure (Steps A–F). Every card shows the exact share of outcomes on that branch, weighted by game probabilities, in which Alabama finishes first or second. A branch ends in “Alabama is in” only when every combination of the games still left on it sends Alabama to Atlanta, and in “Alabama is out” only when none does.

    Which game the chart branches on next is chosen automatically as the one that best separates Alabama-in from Alabama-out outcomes (largest information gain), after Alabama’s own two games. You can branch on a different game from the panel beside the chart. The same set of outcomes can be split in many orders, so the chart is one clear route through all of them, not the only one.

    Step E needs scores from games not yet played and Step F is a random draw, so outcomes that come down to either are shown separately and counted as neither in nor out.

    Win probabilities: the seven Week 11 games ranked teams play in use ESPN’s published FPI figures (Nov. 11). The other 13 games are estimates from ESPN FPI team ratings, P(home) = Φ((ratinghome − ratingaway + 2.2) / 14), the formula that reproduced ESPN’s published post-Week 9 figures within 0.9 points on average. Post-Week 10 ratings were published for Georgia, Alabama, Texas and LSU; the rest were reconstructed by least squares from ESPN’s Week 11 spreads, post-Week 10 rankings, and post-Week 9 ratings, and fit the published figures within 0.04 points. Treat the estimated games as approximate.
